# Mathematical foundation

The golden ratio is the unique strictly positive real solution of
`phi**2 - phi - 1 = 0`: `phi = (1 + sqrt(5))/2`.
The other root is negative. Phi is irrational and algebraic of degree two.

For `a > b > 0`, a golden division satisfies `(a+b)/a = a/b = phi`.
Setting `x=a/b` gives `x=1+1/x`, then `x**2-x-1=0`.
For usable total `L`, the larger part is `L/phi` and the smaller `L/phi**2`.
Their shares are approximately 61.8034% and 38.1966%.

A golden rectangle has long-side/short-side ratio phi. Removing the square built
on its short side leaves a similar rectangle after rotation.
Ratios of consecutive Fibonacci numbers converge to phi; finite ratios and rounded
spacing tokens are approximations, not exact identities.

Name the measured lengths, use consistent units and declare rounding tolerances.
Distinguish the whole canvas from usable space after margins and gutters. Ratios
of absolute coordinates are not ratios of lengths.

Quarter-circle arcs drawn in successive squares illustrate an approximation of a
spiral. A golden rectangle, its subdivisions and an exact logarithmic spiral are
different objects. Do not add a visible grid or spiral unless requested.

These mathematical statements do not guarantee beauty or a psychological response.
